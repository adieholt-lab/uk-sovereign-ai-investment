Wider-industry assumptions — inspect or change

These are explicit illustrative assumptions, not estimates proved by the source studies. Export, retention and productivity inputs describe a reference scenario with 25 percentage points of additional UK market share. Their applied values scale with the capture control; the household percentage is applied to the resulting activity in both scenarios.

Applied at this capture level: extra exports £20.0bn/year by 2045; UK-stack retention +10 percentage points by 2040 (capped); additional productivity £0.0bn/year by 2045. Household rate: 0%, applied to both scenarios.

“Test capture alone” sets additional exports, extra AI-stack retention, household circulation and extra productivity to zero. Supply-chain value is included in both scenarios.

Linked industrial-development assumption

Development factor = additional UK market-share gain / 25 percentage points. Additional capture is the displayed 2040 UK share minus the starting UK share. It represents the scale of assumed additional investment-led development, not a separate investment budget. At zero gain, additional domestic sales, export sales, retention uplift and productivity are all zero, so the two GVA paths coincide. Existing UK activity and its growth remain in both scenarios.

Reference export, retention and productivity assumptions are multiplied by this factor before their existing time profiles and accounting limits are applied. The factor is based on the selected 2040 gain, not multiplied a second time by the annual capture ramp. Supply-chain and household effects are then recalculated from activity. This proportional linkage is a scenario assumption, not evidence of causation or of the investment required.

2040 targets and the 2041–2045 continuation

Market-share capture and the capture-scaled AI-stack retention uplift reach their selected targets in 2040, 15 years after 2025, subject to their limits. The headline comparison and default component table also use 2040.

The market-capture increase is the chosen percentage-point gain multiplied by (year − 2025) / 15, capped at the starting foreign share. The AI-stack retention increase follows the same timing, capped at 90p industry GVA per £1 so that the separate 10p non-AI supply component never takes production GVA above revenue. Targets are milestones, not a freeze: the same trends continue from 2041 to 2045 until these limits are reached. An eventual change in slope at a physical limit is intentional.

Total UK expenditure and existing export/other activity keep compounding at their selected annual growth rates. The additional-export and optional productivity inputs use their separately labelled 2045 targets. Neither the 2040 milestone nor the later extrapolation is a validated forecast.

How the model reconciles

Let S be total UK customer spending, o the starting foreign share, and c the progressively captured share. The no-intervention customer revenues are DA = S(1 − o) and FA = So. With investment they are DB = DA + Sc and FB = FA − Sc. Both pairs add to S.

R is the calibrated export/other revenue proxy, grown at the fourth slider's rate. E is capture-scaled additional export revenue. Let v = 0.55 plus the phased, capture-scaled increase in domestic AI-stack retention. Industry GVA A = 0.55(DA + R) + 0.15FA. Industry GVA B = v(DB + R + E) + 0.15FB. Thus captured sales initially add only 55p − 15p = 40p net UK industry GVA per £1, before deeper retention.

Non-AI supply-chain GVA = 0.10 × UK-led revenues + 0.03 × foreign-led revenues, separately in each scenario. Industry and non-AI production GVA per £1 cannot sum above £1: deeper stack retention is capped accordingly. No supplier turnover is added on top of these GVA amounts.

Optional household GVA = the selected additional percentage of industry + supply-chain GVA, applied to both scenarios. It is defined to exclude imports, taxes, savings, displaced activity and value already counted above; it is a sensitivity assumption, not a validated multiplier. Optional productivity is a capture-scaled annual increment over the import-led counterfactual, ramping after five years, net of AI input costs and excluded from all earlier components. Its reference target is multiplied by the development factor once; household effects are not multiplied by that factor again.

Wider modelled UK GVA = industry GVA + non-AI supply-chain GVA + optional household GVA + optional additional productivity. This is not the total UK economy, nor a comprehensive valuation of every benefit of AI. The 2025 industry subtotal equals £18.6bn at the reference 65% overseas share; the wider total is modelled, not a published national-account statistic. Both scenarios have a zero 2025 difference.

Important limits

These calculations assume the expenditure reference can be treated as end-customer spending, excluding transactions between AI suppliers. Published expenditure and sector GVA have different coverage; the calibration is illustrative, not a reconciliation of observed national accounts. Starting market shares are sensitivity settings; changing them does not rewrite actual 2025 GVA.

Ownership and production location are different. GVA credits UK production even when a company is foreign-owned. Profit remittances affect national income and cannot simply be deducted from UK GVA. Tax receipts, wages and reinvested profits are not added again; investment costs and financial returns are not modelled.

Annual amounts are nominal scenario £bn; growth rates include any assumed price changes. The comparison-panel cumulative gap sums 2026–2040 annual differences without discounting. The chart and CSV continue through 2045. It is not ROI, fiscal payback or a Green Book benefit-cost ratio. Supplier and household effects are gross contribution sensitivities; national displacement, capacity constraints and the opportunity cost of funding have not been fully appraised. The Green Book cautions against treating Keynesian multipliers as social benefits in option appraisal [5].
